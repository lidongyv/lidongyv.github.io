NeurIPS 2025

3D Visual Illusion Depth Estimation

Chengtang Yao, Zhidan Liu, Jiaxi Zeng, Lidong Yu, Yuwei Wu, Yunde Jia

Problem
3D visual illusions — paintings, screens and mirrors that disguise a flat surface as a solid object — fool human vision. Do they fool machine depth estimation too? How badly do monocular, stereo and multi-view methods fail on illusion scenes, and can the common sense of a vision-language model correct them?
Method
Built a 3D-Visual-Illusion dataset of ~3k scenes and 200k images (synthetic plus real stereo captured with an Intel RealSense L515, illusion-region masks from SAM plus human annotation); proposed a VLM-guided monocular–stereo fusion framework in which the vision-language model decides where to trust the monocular prior and where to trust disparity, fusing the two depth sources adaptively.
SOTA monocular models break down inside real illusion regions (DepthPro+align bad3 34.98%, AbsRel 0.09; Marigold bad3 59.67%), and stereo models are equally fooled by mirrors and transparency (RAFT-Stereo bad3 13.20%, EPE 1.62). The fusion method reaches EPE 1.77 / bad5 3.60 inside illusion regions while taking the lowest zero-shot EPE 1.50 on standard Middlebury (RAFT-Stereo 1.92, Selective-RAFT 2.34, StereoAnything 2.89).

Why it matters One delivery containing dataset, benchmark, method and open weights — with an explicit statement of where foundation models systematically fail in safety-relevant scenes. That is exactly the judgement an AD perception lead needs.

ICCV 2025Oral

Diving into the Fusion of Monocular Priors for Generalized Stereo Matching

Chengtang Yao, Lidong Yu, Zhidan Liu, Jiaxi Zeng, Yuwei Wu, Yunde Jia

Problem
Fusing the unbiased monocular priors of visual foundation models (DepthAnything V2 and friends) into stereo matching helps in occlusions and non-Lambertian surfaces, but naive fusion hits three hard problems: relative depth does not align with absolute disparity; iterative updates become over-confident and fall into local optima; and noisy early-iteration disparities mislead the fusion.
Method
A binary local ordering map converts the depth map into a binary relative representation that unifies relative and absolute depth; the same map re-weights the initial disparity update to suppress local optima and early noise; and the final monocular-depth-to-disparity fusion is formulated as a registration problem solved by a per-pixel linear regression module for globally adaptive alignment.
Generalizing from SceneFlow to Middlebury / ETH3D cuts error by more than half and improves Booster Bad-x.0 by about 10 points without extra stereo data or augmentation, at essentially unchanged runtime. DrivingStereo rainy EPE is 1.29 versus 1.80 (RAFT-Stereo), 2.32 (IGEV) and 2.22 (Selective-IGEV).

Why it matters An ICCV 2025 Oral and a template for actually landing foundation-model priors in a geometric task: the training, evaluation and visualization pipeline is fully reproducible and directly transferable to end-to-end perception teams.

ICCV 2023

Sparse Point Guided 3D Lane Detection

Chengtang Yao, Lidong Yu, Yuwei Wu, Yunde Jia

Problem
3D lane detection usually builds a dense front-view ↔ BEV correspondence, but lanes occupy a tiny fraction of the image and most of that correspondence is redundant background — so dense high-resolution correspondence becomes the compute bottleneck.
Method
A coarse-to-fine two-stage design: the coarse stage builds a dense-but-cheap front-view ↔ BEV correspondence at very low resolution to get coarse lanes; the fine stage samples sparse points along those lanes, pulls local features from the high-resolution front-view feature map and refines the 3D lanes hierarchically in BEV space.
F1 52.3% on the OpenLane validation split (PersFormer 50.5%); per-scene analysis gives overall F1 53.7, 3.2 points above the SOTA at the time and up to +6.2 on hard scenes — with roughly half the memory and about 2× the inference speed.

Why it matters A textbook industrial design that trades sparsity for compute, directly matching the compute budget constraints of production lane and road-structure perception.

ICCV 2023

Parameterized Cost Volume for Stereo Matching

Jiaxi Zeng, Chengtang Yao, Lidong Yu, Yuwei Wu, Yunde Jia

Problem
Large disparity ranges explode the cost of stereo matching; dynamic cost volumes only look at a local disparity neighbourhood and lack global view, so they need many iterations to approach the truth.
Method
Observing that a dynamic cost volume approximates a single fixed-small-variance Gaussian, the work parameterizes the whole disparity space with a mixture of Gaussians: each pixel's disparity distribution is given by weights, means and variances; means and variances sample disparity candidates and compute cost, weights and means produce the output. Parameters are optimized through JS divergence, implemented as gradient descent inside a feed-forward module.
SceneFlow EPE 0.62 at 62 ms per frame (RAFT-Stereo 0.65 / 254 ms — about 4× faster); on KITTI 2015 it is 7× faster than RAFT-Stereo and only 0.24% D1-all behind CREStereo while being 7–8× faster; on Middlebury nearly 15× faster than RAFT-Stereo; Booster Bad2 27.41% vs 38.66%.

Why it matters Pushes the accuracy–speed Pareto frontier forward as a whole, and is a drop-in module for real-time stereo depth.

CVPR 2022

FoggyStereo: Stereo Matching With Fog Volume Representation

Chengtang Yao, Lidong Yu

Problem
Fog scattering blurs images and makes matching ambiguous. Prior work removes fog as if it were noise — but fog itself carries depth cues.
Method
Uses the atmospheric scattering model in reverse, from the rendering viewpoint: for each disparity candidate's depth, remove the fog within that depth range and re-render. The clearer the re-rendered image, the closer that depth is to the truth. Images rendered at different depths are stacked along channels into a fog volume and fused with the cost volume to disambiguate fog-corrupted matching.
Improves 10–30% over the SOTA of the time on foggy scenes while holding performance on clear ones; on KITTI 2012 synthetic fog it is 30% better than DeepPruner and ~10% better than PSMNet, and on SceneFlow fog nearly 3× more accurate than DeepPruner.

Why it matters With a single stereo pair and no extra foggy supervision it turns bad weather from a penalty into a source of information — the classic low-cost robustness play in production perception.

Deep Stereo Matching With Explicit Cost Aggregation Sub-Architecture

Lidong Yu, Yucheng Wang, Yuwei Wu, Yunde Jia

Problem
Research on deep stereo matching concentrated on feature extraction and similarity measurement, while the equally critical cost aggregation step was left to fixed windows or implicit networks.
Method
Reframes cost aggregation as a learning problem of generating and then selecting aggregation proposals, implemented as a two-stream sub-architecture: one stream generates candidate aggregations, the other selects among them using low-level structural cues from a lightweight CNN. This gives cost aggregation a global view and corrects errors caused by the local receptive field of cost computation.
On SceneFlow: error>1px 8.93% (GC-Net 11.3%), error>3px 5.62% (7.2%), MAE 1.75 px (2.21 px); KITTI 2015 D1-all 2.79% (GC-Net 2.87%, PSMNet 2.38%).

Why it matters First-author work, AAAI spotlight, and one of the early papers to make cost aggregation explicitly learned — evidence that since my master's I have been working on the least glamorous but most accuracy-critical part of stereo matching.

arXiv 2020Preprint

Content-Aware Inter-Scale Cost Aggregation for Stereo Matching

Chengtang Yao, Yunde Jia, Huijun Di, Yuwei Wu, Lidong Yu

Problem
Multi-scale cost aggregation downsamples the cost volume to gain context, then inevitably loses detail when upsampling back. How can upsampling both restore detail and aggregate cross-scale information?
Method
Learns content-aware dynamic filter weights so every position in the fine-scale cost volume adaptively aggregates from the coarse one; a decomposition strategy first learns a 2D similarity from the two feature maps and then constructs the 3D filter weights from it, splitting joint 3D space–disparity aggregation into 1D disparity plus 2D space and cutting 3D-convolution cost sharply.
SceneFlow EPE 0.57, state of the art at the time and roughly twice as good as the PSMNet baseline; close to SOTA on KITTI 2015. Ablations replacing PSMNet's bilinear / deconvolutional upsampling with this method improve EPE by 0.48 / 0.33 respectively.

Why it matters Splitting 3D cost aggregation into 1D disparity plus 2D space remains a directly useful idea for efficient cost-aggregation design.

arXiv 2023Tech report

DRL-Based Trajectory Tracking for Motion-Related Modules in Autonomous Driving

Yinda Xu, Lidong Yu

Problem
Trajectory tracking is a basic routine for planning and control modules; classical methods make strong assumptions about scenes and dynamics models and lose robustness as soon as reality deviates.
Method
Learns the tracking policy directly with deep reinforcement learning — model-free and data-driven, gaining robustness and accuracy from representation power plus RL exploration. The policy composes with several dynamics models (bicycle model and others) and can be chained with iLQR post-optimization.
At 5 m/s with a bicycle model and zero optimization iterations, mean tracking error is 0.0981 versus 0.2555 for pure pursuit + iLQR — a 61.6% relative reduction; the gain grows at higher initial speeds (20 / 30 m/s). Robust to noise and adaptable across dynamics models.

Why it matters One of the few public works that crosses from perception into planning and control and explains how to combine an RL policy with a classical optimizer in engineering terms.
